Never use em dashes (—) in any prose I write for the user to share, post, or send: LinkedIn copy, outreach emails, README sections, marketing text, social drafts.

**Why:** Em dashes are a strong tell of LLM-generated writing in 2025-2026 copywriting culture. The user is actively trying to make their outreach and social posts look human-written, and explicitly flagged "remove long hyphens that humans do not use". See related: [[feedback_abstract_magnitudes_not_counts]] for the same theme of "don't make the writing look brittle / automated".

**How to apply:** Replace each — with a comma, a period (split into two sentences), parentheses, a colon, or restructure. Regular hyphens (`-`) in compounds (open-source, 90-second, follow-up, real-time) are fine; humans use those constantly. En dashes in number ranges (2026–2027) are also fine. Only the long em dash needs scrubbing. Code comments and tool output are exempt — only prose intended for human readers counts.
